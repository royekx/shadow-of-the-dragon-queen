High Hill was silent afterward. Bodies lay scattered across the hill, militia and mercenary alike, while Becklin gave orders and survivors were carried back toward town. Cudgel's fury burned hot. One of the traitorous mercenaries had been kept alive, and she wanted answers from him badly enough that she could barely hold herself back. Becklin, though furious herself, kept her composure. Anger, she made clear, could not be allowed to make the next decision for them.

The walk back into Vogler was grim. On the way Becklin told them the hill was one of two attacks. Assassins had gone after the town's leaders and Cudgel at the same time, and that was what had sent her and Cudgel up to High Hill. Festival banners still hung across the town, cheerful and bright, while bloodied survivors were brought beneath them. Villagers who had not seen the battle came out pale-faced and horrified, then immediately began helping where they could. The contrast was cruel: only hours before, Vogler had been celebrating its history; now it was living through the beginning of another. Becklin asked the party to stay and help in case the town was attacked again. They agreed to remain until Vogler was secure and could stand on its own.

At the Brass Crab, the captured mercenary was awakened and questioned. Cudgel nearly lost herself to rage, shaking and striking him, but Mayor Raven stepped in before the interrogation became nothing but vengeance. Aratos leaned close and made the threat clear in a low voice. The man finally broke. He claimed he did not know everything, only that Cudgel's lieutenant, Gragonis, had gone out to the woods a few days earlier and returned with gold. Gragonis had then recruited some of the mercenaries to betray Cudgel. The coin had been good enough, and for men with weak loyalty, that was enough.

The name struck Cudgel hard. Gragonis was her lieutenant, the big one: the huge half-ogre the party had killed on High Hill. The betrayal was personal. One of the party made a fist, lightning crackled in the air around it, and the prisoner gave up the rest. The job had been to kill the militia and then take the rest of Vogler. Cudgel had simply been in the way. The surviving traitors had likely retreated toward the mercenary camp. He knew only that Gragonis had gone somewhere out past that camp to get the gold.

Cudgel said her camp lay about an hour outside town. She insisted on going back that night herself to see who remained loyal and who had turned. The party considered sending someone with her, fearing a trap, but she refused a direct escort. Her people were her responsibility. She meant to slip in, look, and come back. Mayor Raven judged that the gold pointed to a second camp in the forest, someone else's, and wanted it found. Darrett was asked to guide the party northeast through the woods, for reconnaissance only.

Kakashi sent his raven to follow Cudgel at a distance, while Aratos, Kakashi, Jasper, and Darrett prepared to scout the woods where the traitorous lieutenant had gone. Roshi and Mayriel were spent from the battle. They stayed in Vogler to guard the town and take turns resting.

Before leaving, the scouts rested briefly, and Aratos slipped into a light sleep. A vision came to him. He saw impossible skies and a colossal tree split between flame and frost. At its base stood a stern, ancient elder who said the time had come and Aratos must choose his path. On a great stone lay the choice: twin blades with chained hilts, bound in fury and vengeance, or an axe with ice along its handle, marked by cold restraint and resolve. The words pressed into him like a command: choose, boy. The gods are watching. When Aratos woke, the chill of the vision remained, and with it a pull in one definite direction.

The scouting party followed Darrett into the woods, though the young squire's confidence occasionally outpaced his sense of direction. After two or three hours of careful travel, with night fallen, they reached the edge of an encampment. Jasper moved ahead alone, vanishing into the dark as only he could. What he found was a true military presence: one to two hundred armed figures, organized and well supplied, waiting in strength.

Hidden against a tree, Jasper listened to a patrol pass. One of its members made animal grunts, and none of them spoke. The patrol was heading toward the spot where his companions waited, so he threw a rock to draw it off. The rock landed where he wanted, but his arm brushed a bush as he lowered it, and one of the patrol came toward him. Thinking quickly, he used the Dragon's Eye coin to disguise himself as one of the soldiers and pretended to be relieving himself against a tree. A guard challenged him, accepted the story and told him to hurry back. The deception was crude and just convincing enough to keep him alive. None of his companions saw it.

Aratos had seen movement in the dark and crept closer to look. He made out a figure too large and broad in the shoulder to be Jasper, with a build that reminded him of the creatures the party had fought on the road. The others had not seen it. Behind him, Kakashi and Darrett lay flat while Kakashi cast the illusion of a boulder over them both. The figure searched for a few moments and walked back the way it had come.

Back in Vogler, Roshi and Mayriel did what they could among the wounded. Roshi laid hands on an injured fisherman, and divine warmth passed through her into him. He looked at her with awe, asking whether she had been touched by the gods. Nothing like it had been seen in centuries, he said. Later, Roshi reflected on Ispin, on the way he had carved himself into the hearts of others, and on whether she had done the same. She remembered his words, that to live is to be known and remembered. When she opened her eyes, a silhouette slipped from the edge of her sight and left a single flower behind. With Ispin's shield near her, his presence felt close.

Mayriel followed a canary through the wounded. It settled on the shoulder of a young woman who was hiding a fatal injury while insisting others needed care more than she did. Mayriel saw through the brave face and healed her with golden divine light. The woman wept in gratitude. When Mayriel asked her about the canary, she was confused and looked to her shoulder. She had seen no bird. Once again the old question emerged: had Mayriel been touched by the gods? Mayriel said she had. Afterward she heard a voice, soothing and commanding, speak one word: good.

Mayriel then went to Mayor Raven and asked about allies. The nearest help was two days away, and the town was too large to evacuate quickly. Mayriel urged her to shore up the defenses, and Raven agreed.

When the scouts returned, the party gathered at Mayor Raven's house. Kakashi's raven was still out, though he could feel that their bond was unbroken. Jasper told Mayor Raven, Becklin, and the others what they had seen. The force in the woods was too large to dismiss and too organized to ignore. The town began strengthening what defenses it could, setting watches, posting hunters on the outskirts as lookouts, and preparing for the possibility that the soldiers would come sooner rather than later.

During a patrol of the town, Jasper heard movement inside General Goods and knocked. He was alone. Inside he found Old Man Jenkins packing to flee, and through the old man's shape he could see the small kender within, filling a bag. Jenkins had no intention of dying for Vogler, and he thought Jasper a fool for staying. He meant to slip away in the night and let the town believe Old Man Jenkins had been taken, and he asked Jasper to tell no one he was going. He called Jasper a fellow Dragon's Eye. When Jasper said the town was good and its people needed help, Jenkins grudgingly gave him aid: two healing potions and some leech arrows, with dried leeches fixed to the heads, meant to latch on and draw life from their targets. As Jasper left, Jenkins told him to remember that old Finbar always takes care of him. He would be gone by morning.

By the end of the night Cudgel had not returned, and neither had Kakashi's raven. The camp in the woods was real. The party stayed, as it had promised Becklin, and took what rest it could.
