The party stayed to help the people of Vogler escape, and they got the villagers down to the boats. Along the way four of them, Aratos, Mayriel, Jasper and Kakashi, went up to Thornwall Keep and helped Than Rookledust clear out the draconians that had broken into his home. Than told them to go on ahead. He had two more things to gather before he left.

The last boats of Vogler strained against the docks as smoke rolled across the river. Behind the fleeing villagers, the town that had welcomed the party in Ispin's name was coming apart beneath the assault of the Red Dragon Army. The evacuation had nearly succeeded when the sky darkened with wings. Someone looked up and cried the word that had not been spoken in Krynn with certainty for centuries: dragon.

The creature swept over the docks with its mouth full of flame. Below it, the last of the villagers and the draconians still clashed near the waterfront, buying whatever seconds they could for the others to escape. Then the dragon breathed. Fire poured across the ground, and those still caught in its path were reduced to ash. The sight of it froze the moment in horror. Everyone on the docks could see it. A dragon had come to Vogler.

From Thornwall Keep, Than Rookledust found his own impossible path to the battle. Trapped on the roof as flames climbed the tower, he launched himself from the gnome-flinger and sailed through the air toward the docks. From up there he saw more than most had time to notice. Smaller winged shapes moved through the smoke, and a humanoid figure rode on the great beast's back. He opened a parachute and, with the stubborn confidence of a gnome who had done this before, landed on his feet behind a house near the water.

Mayriel answered the terror with light. She lifted her holy symbol, and a twilight radiance spread from her, wrapping her companions in a dim, protective glow. Jasper took to the air with the winged boots, rising above the docks and loosing an arrow at the dragon. His first shot faltered under the impossible sight of the creature, but he forced his fear into focus and struck true. Roshi raised her bow as well, though the moving beast was difficult to mark through smoke and flame. Kakashi blurred himself with magic and slipped toward cover, preparing for whatever came next.

Then the dragon passed low and its rider jumped down. Green-skinned and orcish, carrying a flaming axe, the warrior looked across the dock and said he had heard on the front lines of some heroes, and that he could not say he saw them. Aratos charged him without hesitation, rage rising as ancestral silhouettes gathered around him: large guardians of his own build and swift, hooded figures circling in silence. His axe bit into the orc's chest. You have a fighter in you, the orc said. Then he gave his name, as a courtesy from one warrior to the person he was about to kill: Theros Flametongue. Flames rose around him, and he swung.

The duel became the heart of the burning dock. Theros struck back with fire and steel while the dragon's body glowed hotter above them. Flame gathered first in its belly, then climbed toward its throat, then burst outward in another sweeping cone. The party scattered as best they could, some finding shelter, others simply enduring the blast. A red draconian came through the smoke, already badly hurt. Than, running toward the fight and listening for anyone left in the houses, shot it in the head. Its body convulsed and exploded, and the blast caught him. Even the fallen enemies were dangerous. Theros hooked Aratos's axe with his own and tore it out of his hands.

Mayriel blessed her companions, and small, peaceful golden dragons appeared around each of them. Roshi put an arrow into the dragon, then vanished and reappeared on safer ground. Jasper, still above the field, watched the dragon carefully and saw what was wrong with it. It moved stiffly, less fluid than a living thing. Machine parts showed along its body, and smoke leaked from it. It was a machine built in the shape of a dragon, worked by a crew in small seats inside its frame. For the moment only he knew.

Kakashi surged forward with lightning in his hand, his Chidori, the sound of a thousand birds screaming through the smoke as he struck Theros in the face with crackling force. Aratos looked at him and smiled, drew his sword, cut into Theros, and scooped his axe back up for a second blow. Theros laughed with the lightning still on him. He struck Aratos again, then rushed through the line at Mayriel, dragging his burning axe along the ground. The blow missed, but its force knocked her flat. Above them the machine breathed again, and Kakashi, Aratos and Jasper were all burned.

Mayriel got to her feet and conjured a spiritual greatsword that hovered near her and cut into him. Jasper told the others what he had seen: the dragon was a machine, and there were people inside working it. He landed on a rooftop and tried a shot at one of the seats, and missed. Roshi's next arrow struck the machine squarely. Theros tried to knock the mace from Mayriel's hand, and she held on. Aratos drank a healing potion and threw himself back at him. Then the machine's third breath caught Theros along with those around him. He bore the flames and buckled for a moment.

Another creature came out of the fray, throwing lightning from its hand. Kakashi turned one bolt aside with a shield of force. At last, Mayriel brought Theros low. She reached out with necrotic force, driving him to one knee as he snarled that he would not die to nothings. Then her spiritual sword drove into his throat. Theros fell to the side, purplish blood spilling across the ground. He looked dead. No one stopped to make sure.

The dragon-machine still burned above them, its belly glowing again. Aratos hurled his javelin of lightning into the sky. As it left his hand, a rough old voice in his ear said one word: good. Only he heard it. Lightning tore through the construct. The strike ripped into its mechanisms, and screams rang out from within as explosions burst across its frame. The dragon's tail spun down, its body failed, and the great machine crashed into the docks in a storm of fire and metal. Jasper, in the air, was just clear of it. Everyone else was caught. Roshi went down, kept from death by the periapt she wore. Aratos fell as well.

Mayriel reached Aratos and healed him. Then she looked at the wreck and saw that one of the crew still lived, at the base of the machine in the seat that had worked the dragon's mouth. Her spiritual sword could not get at him through the half-melted metal, and Jasper's arrow narrowly missed. Kakashi walked up to the wreck, found the gap where he could reach a hand in, and released his Chidori again. The last operator wailed and then exploded, and the blast dropped Kakashi as well. As the lightning left his hand and the flames went up around him, he remembered something he had pushed away long ago: a village set aflame, people burning and crying and falling. The memory was his alone.

The lightning-throwing creature was still on the field. Aratos threw his javelin at it. This time cold crept from his chest down his arm, and a voice asked: are you worthy? For a moment the javelin took another shape in the air. He could not hold the connection, and it changed back and flew past its mark. That too was his alone. Than poured a potion into Kakashi. A bolt from the creature struck Mayriel and broke her blessing, and she turned to heal Kakashi.

From the air, Jasper searched the smoke for more enemies. He saw a man running hard toward the docks with someone on his back, and recognized the one being carried as Darrett Highwater. He also saw a faint shimmer that no one else could see: Finbar. Then he shot the creature through. It burst apart before it hit the ground, as the red draconian had, and the blast caught Than a second time.

With no more enemies in sight, the party gathered what they could. Kakashi searched the wreck among scrap and charred bodies and found a small glowing gem. It burned his hand when he took it, and he judged that it was where the machine's fire had come from. Aratos took Theros's axe from beside the body. It was no longer aflame, but heat still came off it.

The runner came into view: Jeyev, Cudgel's bald lieutenant, carrying Darrett. Than fed Roshi a potion, and she woke to find him standing over her with a hint of alcohol on his breath. Jeyev told Jasper that Becklin and Cudgel were still fighting and had ordered him to take Darrett and run. Vogler was being occupied. Some of the party thought about going back to fight. The houses were empty, and Becklin had charged them with getting the villagers out. Kakashi said they should leave, help the villagers who had already escaped, and spread word of what they had seen. One of them asked whether the others believed the vision now.

Finbar, visible to Jasper alone, looked at him, looked at the boat, and made for it. Than ran ahead to ready the boat, and he left Vogler aboard it with the party. As the others came down to the water, they all heard a voice from nowhere say it was glad to see they were not dead yet. Finbar came aboard too, and none of the others saw him.

Mayriel was still on the dock, trying to heal the others, when she felt a divine pull deepen in her chest. It wanted more of her. It wanted her to submit to Paladine. She offered up her family signet ring and swore to follow him. With the oath came an understanding: one day he would ask something of her, and she would have to do it. The ring stayed on her hand and took on a faint divine glow. The golden canary she had seen several times before flew into view. Golden light spread from it and healed them all, and the bird grew larger as it shone.

Jasper took one last look from the air. More soldiers were moving into the heart of the town, and ahead of them a fight was moving through the streets faster than anything the party could match. He landed on the boat and said only that it looked bad out there.

The boat pulled away as flames, smoke and soot covered Vogler. They looked back one more time at the village that had welcomed them when their friend had passed. Through the smoke, they saw three figures moving toward the docks. Becklin Uth Viharin and Cudgel Ironsmile were fighting a taller woman, lanky, with charred skin, blackened armor, and a massive sword, and they were struggling to hold her back. The woman kicked Cudgel aside, and Cudgel went through the wall of a building. Becklin fought on, parrying and pushing off draconians until she reached the edge of the dock. She looked out at the departing boat, resigned to her fate and grateful to them.

One of them loosed an arrow toward the enemy, but it flew too far and struck the ground. Becklin raised her hands as if to say it was all right. Two slashes crossed her back, and she fell into the dark water.

The charred woman stood at the edge of the dock. Two more figures came up out of the smoke. One stood beside her. The other stood over the body of Theros Flametongue, and flame passed across him until he stood up once more. The woman reached out toward the boat. Run as far as you can, she said. You will not escape the flames of the Dragon Queen.

They made their way down the Vingaard River, safe for the moment and unsure what waited ahead, with Vogler burning in the distance. One of them said they ought to be proud of what they had done, and that they were more afraid than ever.
