Morning came with little rest for Vogler's leaders. Mayor Raven looked exhausted, pacing through her home. At the Brass Crab, Becklin and Darrett studied a map of the town, marking places that could be reinforced if the army came. Then Kakashi's raven returned to the window, pecking for entry with news of Cudgel. It reported that Cudgel had found her mercenary camp, that some had been captured, and that she was returning soon. Pressed for more, it added that she was not being followed and that someone was with her.

Cudgel arrived with a shaved-headed man in plate armor and blue-gray robes. She introduced him as Jeyev, her lieutenant. Her report confirmed the worst. The army encampment was real, positioned to the northwest, and the remaining mercenaries were still loyal to her. Some traitors had been captured, but the danger was far larger than a broken mercenary company. Cudgel laid out the hard truth before Becklin, Raven, and the party: Vogler could stay and fight, or it could evacuate.

Mayor Raven resisted the thought of abandoning the town. Vogler was home. But the party pressed the need for a plan beyond pride. Becklin took some of them aside and agreed that escape had to be prepared, even if it was a last resort. In her experience an attack was likely. She would try to convince Raven, and Raven would have to convince the people. The river and boats offered a possible way out. The vessels would need patching, the fishermen might lend their own, and between three and four hundred people would have to be carried. Cudgel offered to send word to her remaining mercenaries, half a day away, so they could help defend the town. Even with them, Vogler could not hold for long. The nearest help was Kalaman, where the Knights of Solamnia were garrisoned, a few days away to the northeast across the river, and as long again to return. Cudgel offered to send one of her men. Kakashi considered sending his raven, though the flight would take a full day and leave him without it.

While the leaders talked, one of the party went looking for potions. Most of the town had shut its doors. At Old Man Jenkins's shop the door was locked, no one answered the knock, and through the window the shelves stood half empty.

Lord Bakaris the Elder then forced his way into the discussion, dressed in red and gold and offended that anyone had dared make plans without him. Becklin, Raven, and Cudgel reacted with immediate irritation, but Aratos and Kakashi tried to turn Bakaris's vanity into usefulness. They hailed him as the savior the town needed and pressed him to carry word to Kalaman in person, as only a man of his rank could. Bakaris, predictably, preferred sending a letter with his seal rather than riding himself into danger. A fine steed and a faster rider could carry it, he said, and Kalaman knew him well. He heard the plan from Becklin, called it adequate, and went to ready his own things.

Then a villager burst in with worse news. A rider in black and red armor waited at the gate, asking for whoever spoke for Vogler. Raven, Becklin, and Cudgel went to meet him, while Roshi broke away toward Thornwall Keep in search of anything that might help fortify the town. Kakashi sent his raven ahead to watch the gate. The rider stayed in the saddle and asked who spoke for Vogler. Raven answered that she did, and the rider handed her a scroll.

Mayor Raven read it aloud. It was a command from the Red Dragon Army, and it named Takhisis, the Dragon Queen. That very night Vogler was to open its doors, quarter the soldiers beneath its roofs, feed them, shelter them, and comply. Refusal would bring swift and final mercy. Becklin asked how long they had. You have till sunset, the rider said. Raven pleaded, and the rider gave her no further answer.

While the messenger waited below, Roshi went alone to Thornwall Keep and met Than, a gnome tinkerer in soot-covered goggles. He welcomed her once she named Becklin, whose home the keep was. He was scattered and hard to keep on one subject, and useful all the same. Roshi asked for help delaying a massacre, and Than admitted he had unstable explosives that might be set as traps, given several pairs of steady hands. He could also help repair boats and perhaps rig a signal flare from the top of the keep, though nothing powerful enough to reach Kalaman without risking the whole town. He asked only that nobody disturb the gnome-flinger on the roof, a machine that could throw a gnome a hundred feet in any direction. From a window of the keep, Roshi spotted several figures posted on the cliffside wearing armor like the messenger's. The rider had not come alone.

Mayor Raven gathered the people of Vogler and told them the truth. The town had been ordered to surrender itself to an unknown army by sunset or face slaughter. Panic broke out almost immediately. Some demanded answers, others begged to flee, and Bakaris pressed at Raven's ear, telling her she needed to make a decision. Kakashi stepped forward, struck his staff, and commanded silence with a flash of power. For a moment the sky itself seemed to darken and turn red, and the crowd stilled. He told them there was a plan, that the mayor and defenders were working together, and that people needed to begin packing and loading the boats. Those who could fight were to stand by. His force gave way to gentleness, and that balance helped turn panic into motion. Villagers began offering boats, labor, and aid.

Roshi returned and told the others what she had seen from the keep. Kakashi's raven circled the cliff and confirmed it. There were four watchers. Three were human soldiers, and the fourth looked more reptilian. Roshi also asked Mayor Raven to send townsfolk up to help Than with his explosives, and Raven agreed. Kakashi left the raven aloft to keep watch.

The party then turned to buying time. It was noon. Cudgel needed to get Jeyev out to warn and gather her loyal mercenaries, but the messenger and cliffside watchers could not be allowed to notice. A plan formed around distraction and misdirection. Roshi and Kakashi would occupy the messenger below. Aratos, Mayariel, and Jasper would deal with the watchers above. Jeyev would ride when an opening appeared.

The cliffside plan fell apart almost immediately. Jasper rose on his winged boots and vanished into position. Aratos climbed the rock bare-handed, then went back down to help Mayariel, and their climb was loud enough to draw attention. Aratos tried to bluff the watcher who came to look, asking how he would attack the place. The man answered that he would push Aratos off the cliff and laugh while he fell. Aratos laughed with him and tried to shove him over the edge, but the attempt failed, and a blade came to his throat. Below, Kakashi asked the messenger to let the women and children leave before the occupation. Submit peacefully, the rider answered, and there will be no need. Roshi stepped in front of Kakashi to hide his casting, and he created the sound of someone screaming for help. Roshi ran toward it to sell the illusion. The distraction was good enough for Jeyev to spur his horse and begin his escape, but the enemy reacted quickly, and battle broke out on both fronts.

On the cliff, Mayariel's first spell dropped one watcher into sleep where he stood, and red lightning from another watcher's hand went wide of her in answer. She then called down a moonlit beam of divine power that burned one of the enemies in radiant light. Jasper struck from hiding and kept repositioning. Aratos raged and fought through burning blades. When he swung his axe he felt it turn cold in his hand. The blow missed, and he heard the elder warrior's voice say one word: prove. Cudgel climbed into the fight and joined them, arriving in time to find one enemy reduced to ash by Mayariel's moonlight. Then the lightning thrower struck Cudgel and Mayariel in quick succession. Mayariel fell unconscious and hovered close to death. Aratos threw his javelin as a bolt of lightning that tore through two of the enemy and jolted the sleeper awake. Jasper put an arrow through another watcher's skull, and the body dropped to the foot of the cliff.

Below, the messenger wheeled to cut Jeyev off. Roshi ran in swinging, and Jeyev turned the rider's sword aside with his own. Kakashi armored himself in magic and pressed forward. Lightning came down from the cliff and burned Roshi where she stood. Jeyev broke away, then turned back to trade blows when it was clear the rider would keep chasing him. Roshi challenged the mounted messenger and drew his attention onto herself. Kakashi closed in and unleashed a crackling blue lightning strike that sounded like a thousand birds. For a brief instant he seemed younger and sharper, a man in his prime. The messenger was badly hurt and still in the saddle.

On the cliff, Aratos gave up his own attack to pour a healing potion into Mayariel, and she woke. Below, Jeyev broke off and rode on. Cudgel crushed one of the watchers. Jasper nocked one of Jenkins's leeching arrows, and the petrified leech at its tip came alive and squirmed as he loosed it into one of the last enemies standing. Aratos then lined up his javelin and hurled it with brutal precision. It struck the messenger through the heart, killing him and sending his horse fleeing. With the last enemies dead, Jeyev was gone into the distance with his message, and the party had bought Vogler a little more time.

Among the fallen, they found two healing potions and some gold, and on one body a magical amulet and a blue-gemmed ring. The amulet was later identified as a Periapt of Wound Closure, which could preserve someone from death when they fell, and the ring as a Ring of Protection. The party had not yet settled who would carry them.

A few hours remained before sunset, and Vogler moved with desperate purpose. Townsfolk repaired boats, carried supplies, helped Than prepare unstable traps, and readied themselves for flight. Cudgel's mercenaries established a position north of town to slow the Red Army. As evening began to fall, Becklin called the party together, told them the Red Army was on its way, and gave them their charge. She and Cudgel would stand at the front line. The party would help the villagers escape and serve as the last line if the defenders broke.

Before she left, Becklin asked a favor of Aratos in front of the others, knowing he had grown close to Darrett. She said she was unsure what would happen. There was a chest in Thornwall Keep, and he was to fetch it and deliver it to Darrett once things settled, whether that came after the battle or after an escape.

As evening fell, the boats were close to ready, and the party remained behind with the task of seeing the people through.
