Beneath the watchful light of Krynn's three moons, Aratos woke from a dream that felt like a summons. In the vision, the red moon stood eclipsed by its two siblings, and scaled warriors marched across the land in ordered ranks. Their weapons were drawn, their purpose certain: they had come to conquer. Aratos stood his ground, and a deep voice in his chest answered: Good. Fight. When he woke, the moons still hung aligned outside his window and the dream did not fade. It lingered like smoke in his lungs, pointing him toward Kalaman and the Knights of Solamnia.

Elsewhere, on the Night of the Eye, Kakashi and Jasper stood among hopeful mages at the Barb, a half-ruined spire where the Mages of High Sorcery held the exam that would begin an apprenticeship. The two were strangers. Jasper carried a purpose of his own beneath his apprentice's mask, and no one there knew it. The trial placed them in a strange chamber of invisible walls and rune-carved stone, with a closed door and an obvious key that seemed almost too inviting to trust. Kakashi hung back and watched while Jasper felt his way through the unseen maze to the runes and recognized a spell of opening. Kakashi confirmed it and judged that the key might be a trap. They left the key, spoke the spell at the far door, and it vanished in a flash of light. A red-robed proctor handed over a moon-marked spellbook and a scroll that sent them east to Kalaman to continue the apprenticeship. They took a caravan east together, Kakashi with one eye open, wondering why a fellow candidate had not known that spell already. Aratos rode the same caravan, and for days the three barely spoke.

Roshi and Mayariel traveled under a different banner: grief. Letters from Becklin Uth Viharin, whom neither had met, had drawn them toward Vogler for the funeral of Ispin Greenshield, an old adventurer who had left lasting marks upon elven memory. The two elves met on a caravan bound for Vogler. One night on the road, they followed strange sounds into the dark. A flash of light blinded them in a clearing strewn with bodies, and when it faded they stood before the remnants of an old holy place. There, among broken statues of the old gods, Mayariel watched the statue of a silver dragon rebuild itself. A voice told her that evil was coming and that it would give her the power to stand against it, and platinum flames rose around her. A great bison statue spoke to Roshi, and a sacred calling settled over her like a hand upon the shoulder: take up arms, evil is coming, gather a party. The memory blurred afterward.

The roads met in violence before any of them reached Vogler. The next day, as the elves' caravan rested, Mayariel's longbow began to hum, and a scream followed. The caravan was ambushed by reptilian humanoids with scaled hides, flat noses, long jaws, and voices that spoke in Draconic. One knight fell with an arrow in his throat, and soon every knight in the caravan was dead. Aratos, Kakashi and Jasper heard the screams from a distance and ran toward them, and in the space of a breath, strangers became allies. Jasper vanished into the terrain and struck from hiding. Kakashi lashed out with lightning. Roshi threw herself bodily into danger, grappling one of the creatures and demanding to know who had sent it. Mayariel alone understood its answer: the Shadow Queen sent us. Aratos recognized the soldiers from his dream and charged into battle with the force of the omen still burning in him. He felled their archer and then their leader, a larger creature that Mayariel had wrapped in fire shaped like a dragon. Roshi's captive tore free. Kakashi sent a raven to carry his lightning to it, and when it ran, Roshi tackled it.

When the last creature was captured, the group pressed it for answers. The others learned what Mayariel had understood, and Aratos told them of his dream and asked them all to help him stop what was coming. The creature answered only in Draconic, and Mayariel translated. It spoke of reclaiming the realm for its queen, laughed that their time was coming, said the east had already fallen, and warned that the shadow was coming fast. Rumors of war far to the east had been easy to dismiss until now. Asked whether more of its kind were near, it swallowed its own tongue and died. None of the five had seen creatures like these before.

Two travelers and a young man who had run for help were all that remained of the caravan. One of them, a woman, asked the five for protection as far as Vogler. It lay on the way to Kalaman, and all five agreed. They buried the fallen and burned the bodies of the scaled attackers. Vogler and Ispin's funeral still lay ahead, and the five of them took the road together.
