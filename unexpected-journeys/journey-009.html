The Reliquary of Lost Oaths

Behind an oath-tested door, a necromancer is raising the knights buried there.

Evening settled over Kalaman as the refugees of Vogler gathered outside the city walls, their tents pitched beneath the shadow of a place that had taken them in. The party returned to Mayor Raven with the council's answer: the people of Vogler would be allowed refuge, though the aid came bound to expectation. Kalaman had troubles of its own, and the party had agreed to lend their strength to the city's defense.

Raven received the news with weary gratitude, and the sight of Fizban walking beside them seemed to calm her. She told them that most of the villagers had been settled as best they could, with tents and fresh water enough for the moment. Food, however, remained a question. Still, she thanked them again and named them honorary Voglerans, a small honor from a people who had lost almost everything except one another. She said they were welcome to stay and share whatever the camp had, and the Voglerans had left a cluster of tents standing for them. The party also warned her that Lord Bakaris had already forced his way into Kalaman's politics. Raven rolled her eyes and said she would try to deal with it.

It was then that Fizban, ever strange and seemingly untouched by ordinary urgency, suggested a remedy for despair: stew. It was hard to build hope on an empty stomach, he said, and Ispin used to make a famous one. He dug through his pockets for the recipe. What he needed was meat. Bear by preference, or deer, or hare, whatever the hills outside the city might offer. He would start the broth himself with a little magic and a local's help. The party, needing something simple after days of fire and grief, set out to hunt with about an hour of daylight left.

Outside Kalaman, the hunt quickly became less simple than intended. Aeimos had returned. He had spent the days since Vogler on the road with Fizban, who took the wrong turn at every fork, and together they had helped several villages and driven reptilian creatures out of some of them. True to his nature, he did not walk openly into the group. He lingered at the edges, moving unseen through the camp and following the others at a distance. Only Jasper noticed him. Jasper gave a small wave and Aeimos answered with a nod, a recognition between two people who understood the value of remaining unseen. Aeimos, pipe in hand and senses sharpened by the strange smoke he drew into his lungs, watched the party move into the wild.

The group split their attention between bear and deer. Roshi, Aratos, Jasper, and Kakashi followed signs of a bear, finding tracks, broken branches, and the places where the animal had passed. Mayariel pursued deer instead, only to find herself separated from the others. Aeimos saw the deer before she did and tried to approach silently to point them out, but the smoke that sharpened his senses had slowed his feet. He stepped too loudly, and Mayariel whirled on him with a dagger drawn. The moment might have been awkward enough on its own, but then Aeimos saw a figure behind her: a pale, beautiful woman with pointed ears and long flowing hair, watching him with unsettling calm. She pointed toward the direction the others had gone, then vanished.

Aeimos could not tell exactly what she was, but he understood the warning. He and Mayariel hurried to the others, where the bear trail had led to a cave. Jasper had already scouted near its mouth and found no bear inside, only a faint blue glow from deeper within. Aeimos greeted his cousin and the rest, then told them that something was wrong and that they should prepare themselves. He said nothing about the woman.

Aeimos went in first. Roshi, Kakashi, and Mayariel followed, while Aratos and Jasper kept watch at the mouth. Inside, the glow pulsed slowly from the back of the cave. Mayariel, looking more closely, noticed purplish-black soot on the floor, and it brought back the purple-flamed things the party had fought on the river earlier that day. Around a bend, the cave opened onto a heavy stone door marked by the symbol of the Knights Radiant, but altered. The familiar sword bore six points instead of four. Roshi knew the order from what Aratos had told her: knights who answered to an oath rather than a crown. Aeimos knew them as wanderers, scattered and without a fixed home, and this was the first place he had ever heard of that was built for them. When one of the party pushed on the door, it stayed shut. Six symbols lit around it, and the air carried a silent demand. It wanted an oath. Kakashi went back for Aratos and Jasper.

Aratos stepped forward first. He felt warmth and storm rise inside him. He whispered the words that shaped him: life before death, strength before weakness, journey before destination. One spoke of the symbol lit, and the word Valor appeared.

Aeimos followed, shaken by the moment and by the path that had led him there. He spoke his own truth beneath his breath, too low for the others to catch more than a little of it: hopelessness is not my privilege. Another spoke lit, and the word Resolve appeared. Tears ran down his face.

Kakashi pieced together what the door was doing. It was looking for the virtues of a knight. Six symbols showed, and only four were correct. As he worked it through, the door seemed to weigh how he thought, and a rune reading Knowledge lit. Aeimos, who knew that protecting people lay at the heart of his order, laid his hand and his head against the stone. He thought of protection as comfort as much as defense, and said: this too shall pass. A third spoke lit. Kakashi then touched the door himself and offered what drove him: always to strive to do the best, and for the greater good. A fourth spoke answered with Foresight. Roshi, watching the spokes light, thought of her own oath, to leave the world a better place than she found it, and felt the spoke of protection resonate with her. A sense of duty came from the stone. The door flashed and opened.

Beyond lay a hidden reliquary. Its passage was lined with murals telling a story none of them had heard before. On one wall, Knights of Solamnia and plainly armored knights, bare of heraldry as the Knights Radiant go, gathered beneath a floating city and fought together against it. Two figures led them: one in Solamnic armor, and one of the plain knights, who sounded a horn. On the other wall the same knights stood in smaller numbers while the city fell burning from the sky. For a long time the Knights of Solamnia had been the only knights the world recognized, so whatever had brought the two orders together had been grave. Someone had recently defaced the horn-bearer's image, smearing it with the same purplish-black ash that marked the cave.

At the end of the hall the way split left and right. Aeimos looked for the pale woman and did not see her. A chill spread from his chest, and a whisper in a language he did not know left him with one meaning: go forward. He turned left. Roshi said that it had taken all of them to open the door and that they should go on together, and Aratos chose to trust his cousin's instinct. The path led to a broken staircase. From below came the clank of metal on stone, a low voice chanting, and a dull purple light. The weapons of warning that some of them carried thrummed faintly. Aeimos descended with almost impossible grace, running along walls and broken stone while the others followed with far less elegance. Roshi struggled and had to be helped down. Kakashi tumbled the rest of the way, landing hard at the bottom.

The chamber beneath was a round tomb of knights. Armored dead stood in place along the walls, their bodies preserved as memorials. In the center stood a knight holding a horn in the crook of his arm. Near him, a withered figure with sunken skin and glowing purple eyes chanted over a spectral black rose with two petals. Two of the knightly dead had already left their places and walked with swords in hand.

The party struck quickly. Kakashi unleashed Witch Bolt, lightning crashing down from above and wracking the chanting figure, but the necromancer did not stop. Jasper glided down with the aid of his boots, put an arrow into the necromancer, and slipped behind a column. The arrow did less harm than it should have. Aeimos saw a thread of power running from the necromancer to the rose, and from the rose into the central knight. He whistled a warning up to Aratos, activated his storm-charged gauntlets, and struck one of the risen knights with lightning from a distance, battering it down before it could close. Mayariel's guiding bolt went wide.

Aratos descended into the chamber and called upon his ancestors. The Emberwake Greataxe felt heavier in his hands than it should have. Fire and storm rose around him as he drew it, and spectral figures appeared at his side: a towering bald warrior, a kingly hooded figure, and a slim blue-hued guardian with a spear. He charged the necromancer, his oath and rage moving together, and drove his weapon into the undead caster. Roshi saw a third petal forming on the rose. She followed with a charge of her own, and her greatsword bit less deeply than she expected before divine power erupted from her blade in the form of radiant rose-thorns.

The necromancer raised a staff of corrupted wood and answered with darkness. A black-purple orb swelled into a sphere of magical night around him, swallowing everyone who stood close. Mayariel stood clear of it. Inside, whispers and wet, monstrous noises filled the air, and a deathly cold gnawed at those trapped within. The necromancer tried to slip away under its cover, but Aratos struck blind and held him where he stood. Along the wall a third knight stepped down from its place. Kakashi maintained his lightning despite the pain, striking the necromancer again through the darkness, then stumbled clear. Jasper slipped free of the shadow and landed a shot that finally broke the necromancer's concentration, causing the orb to vanish. When the light returned, the knight Aeimos had felled was on its feet again, and three of the dead now stood against them.

But the ritual had already gone far enough. A purple shadow was gathering around the central statue.

Aeimos knocked the necromancer down and pummeled him into the ground. The creature cackled and gasped his final words: his will is done, Lord Soth. The rose's dark power surged into the central knight, and the honored dead rose as a wraith. The ritual had not been completed, but it had succeeded enough to corrupt what lay in the reliquary. The black rose dissolved, and the knight's spirit emerged in violet shadow, armored and terrible.

Mayariel burned it at once with sacred flame and called a spiritual weapon to her side. Then the wraith screamed. Sorrow and fear rolled through the room, and most of the party found they could not take a step toward it. Kakashi raised grasping vines charged with death magic. They hurt one of the dead knights and did nothing at all to the wraith, and soon afterward he shook the fear off. Aeimos, held back by a dread that Knights Radiant could be corrupted, studied the chamber instead. The rose had room for five petals and had held only three, one for each knight raised, when the necromancer ended the ritual early with his dying breath. The wraith was dangerous, but not as powerful as it might have become. He judged, too, that the necromancer's purple flame was the same power the others had described from the river. Radiant power would wound the wraith most deeply. He called that knowledge to the others.

Mayariel answered. Her sacred flame burned against the wraith again, and it recoiled from the light, hurt far more than so small a spell should have hurt it. Aratos turned and cut deep into one of the skeletal knights, which stayed on its feet. The wraith struck Jasper and then Mayariel with a necrotic touch that left each of them feeling their very life force diminished. Roshi, unable to close with the wraith, cut at the dead knights with her moon-touched sword and called out a champion's challenge that kept the wraith from moving far from her. Kakashi surged forward with lightning screaming like a thousand birds as he drove it into the wraith and forced the Boilerdrake Heart beyond its limits, adding fire to the strike. Jasper, nearly spent, drank a healing potion and kept shooting. Aeimos closed with storm-lit fists and spent the full charge of his gauntlets, his oath hardening against the wraith's promise that his light would fade.

At last, Mayariel raised her divine power against the undead, and two of the three dead knights turned to flee. Her spiritual weapon became the killing blow. It took the shape of a rose, its petals unfolding into dagger-like blades before closing around the wraith like a divine snare. The shadow was snuffed out, and the dead knights fell. The corrupted spirit gave way to the knight as he had been, noble and confused, bearing a spectral shield and horn.

He thanked them for their bravery and for their help in dispatching this evil. To Aratos and Aeimos, he named them children of Cathan and told them they had proven themselves. Then he raised his horn and blew. The spirits of the three knights who had been twisted into undeath rose from their fallen forms and returned to their places of rest, and their bodies crumbled to ash. The knight faded, the purple glow went out, and the chamber grew still again.

In the aftermath, the party searched what remained. Only the necromancer's body was left. It yielded an intricate armor of bone and a small sum of gold. Kakashi's magic showed the armor steeped in necrotic energy, which he did not count as evil in itself, and the party took it. Aratos cut off the necromancer's head, mindful of a dead enemy who had risen before, and the party kept it to show the city what waited outside its walls. A faint horn call drew the Stormblessed cousins to the central statue. Carved figures moved along the horn in its arm: someone raised it, and more knights appeared. Aeimos lifted it and blew, though no sound came to mortal ears. Two spectral knights appeared, armored and waiting for command. Kakashi's magic read the horn as a thing made to call aid. He asked that Aratos be the one to carry it, since Aeimos might not stay. On the statue was the knight's name: Sir Cthondor.

Before leaving, Aeimos bowed in respect. This was the first true reliquary of the Knights Radiant he had ever known. Aratos laid a hand at the statue's feet. The other passage proved to be a second stair, whole and easy, and the sealed door opened for them from within. Outside, Aeimos looked once more for the pale woman. He did not see her, but the whisper came again, and this time he understood the words: there are more, and he wishes to take them. He kept it to himself. The party, almost absurdly, returned to the hunt they had set out on. The deer eluded them. They found a bear after all and dragged it back to camp.

Fizban, seemingly unaware of the battle beneath the hill, greeted the bear as though the party had simply taken longer than expected, and gave cheerful advice on how to skin it. With help from the Vogler refugees, the meat was prepared, and Ispin's stew came together over the fire. Kakashi told Fizban they needed to talk after dinner about why they had been late. Fizban agreed that they should catch up, and said he would be in Kalaman only another day or so, since many people needed to hear what was happening. Whether the recipe was truly Ispin's or simply another of Fizban's wandering claims mattered little. The people ate, and for one night at least, warmth and food softened the edges of loss. Darrett eventually joined them, covered in sweat and dirt from training, and the camp shared what comfort it could.

As the camp settled for the night, Aeimos left the horn with Aratos and slipped away again, as was his way. One of the others glimpsed him leaving with a bowl of stew in hand. Aratos looked for him but found only absence and the sense that his cousin would be all right. Kakashi asked around for what he would need to call his raven back. Jasper, meanwhile, found a folded paper lying beside him, written in Thieves' Cant and marked with the symbol of the Dragon's Eye. The message was simple and dangerous: "We need to talk."

Beyond the camp, alone, Aeimos saw the pale woman once more. She seemed to mean him no harm, though her purpose stayed hidden. This time he approached her and asked for guidance. She told him that Lord Soth wished to corrupt all knights, their image and their legacies, in vengeance for how they had portrayed him. He would continue until his curse was lifted. The name was only faintly familiar to Aeimos, and the curse was new to him. When he asked where to go, she pointed into the distance. When he looked back, she was gone.

The campfires of Kalaman burned behind him. Aeimos had told no one what he had seen or heard. He understood that Aratos had his path, and he had his own.
