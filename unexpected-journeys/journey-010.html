Unexpected Journey 010

The Weight of the Axe

A recipe hides a spell. A trophy hides a curse.
UJ 010 Party Level 4 Kalaman
Previous JourneyAll JourneysNext Journey
Fizban's stew recipe turns out to be a cipher for a spell. Finbar takes Jasper to meet the head of the Dragon's Eye in Kalaman. In a sparring match the trophy axe's rage overtakes Aratos and he nearly kills a squire. Marshal Vendri sends the party, recorded as the Embers, to find the tinkerer Tatina Rookledust. On the way out, at the reliquary, Aratos holds the axe's fire back through resolve.

The evening after the reliquary settled gently over the refugee camp, and for a little while the world felt almost at peace. Fizban's stew had done its work. The people of Vogler moved through the firelit dark with lighter steps, and the voices around the fires were warm. Fizban himself sat at the fire with his pipe, nodding in and out of sleep with the contented air of a man who had done exactly what he intended. Around him the party shared out what they had carried from the reliquary. Kakashi asked for the Armor of the Fallen, since he had the least protection of any of them, and the others agreed.

Darrett Highwater came in late, worn out from a day of learning his new station. He nodded to them and took his rest among the Voglerans rather than behind Kalaman's walls. Mayor Raven came by to say her goodnights. Then Fizban roused himself. He judged the evening a success and thanked the party for it. He said he might be in Kalaman another day or so, trying to learn what was going on outside the city, and that he was sure their paths would cross again. Then he went in through the gates.

As he shuffled off, a scrap of parchment fell from where he had been sitting and drifted to the ground unnoticed. Roshi retrieved it. The paper turned out to be a recipe: Ispin Greenshield's campfire stew, recorded by Fizban, with a list of fifteen ingredients. At the bottom was a reminder he had written to himself. Do not forget the fire, the blade, or the green bits. The last time he had forgotten one of them, and the stew had become extremely educational. Roshi sat down to study it.

Aratos sat apart from the others on a tree stump, sharpening his weapons by firelight in the methodical way of a man who needed his hands busy while his mind worked. As the stone moved against steel, the campfire shifted. Flames bent and gathered into the shape of a great tree, its branches burning on one side and glazed with frost and ice on the other. At the base of the tree, a small black rose bloomed for just a moment before vanishing. For that moment Aratos felt as he had in the cave, where the party had seen black roses before. Beyond the vision of the tree, a figure stood in the distance, wearing a dark green hood. Aratos rose and walked toward him. The man was hard to keep in focus. He was human, a little older than Aratos, gruff-bearded, and he carried himself like a man hiding his own regality.

Aratos recognized him as the king, one of the ancestral spirits he had glimpsed before. The king spoke only a few words. The same blood flows through my veins. The same weakness. Don't let it take you. Aratos looked away for a moment, and the figure faded. He knew what the words pointed to: the history of his family, and the failure of his ancestor Cathan Twice-Born, which had brought about ruin. As he thought on it, the Emberwake axe warmed a little in his hand. He walked to the place where the figure had stood and found the grass undisturbed. The others saw him walk out and bend over the grass. He said nothing of it, and returned to his weapons and his contemplation.

Roshi, by then, had read enough to share. She told the others that Fizban's reminder kept coming back to a fire, a blade and green bits. Put together, the words made a name: green flame blade. Mayriel drew their eyes to the note at the bottom of the page. The recipe listed its components in absurd quantities, each line bearing a number far beyond what any ordinary stew would need. Kakashi studied the numbers and recognized a pattern in them. Wizards were known to protect their spells, hiding them so that only those who knew how to read them could draw them out. The numbers, he suspected, were not measurements at all.

Jasper had other business. During the feast someone had slipped a note into his pocket, and it said they needed to talk. He looked around the camp and found Finbar standing a little way outside it, invisible to everyone else. The shimmer around him was familiar enough that Jasper caught it, and Finbar gave him a nod. Jasper got up and walked out to him. Someone would like to meet you, Finbar said, and he recommended that Jasper come now. Jasper agreed. Finbar dropped his invisibility, and the weathered face of Old Man Jenkins was gone. In its place he wore the look of a human man with slightly tanned skin, one the knights at Kalaman's gates had seen come through before. The others, still passing the recipe around, saw Jasper leave the fire and head into the city. They did not make out who he went with.

The streets were calm in the late evening, lit by the pale light of the moons. Finbar brought Jasper to an establishment whose sign read Peller's Provisions, a general goods store that drew little attention. When Jasper looked closely at the sign, he saw the mark of a dragon's eye at the end of the name, and a shimmer resolved the name into another one: Finn's Bar. Inside the closed shop Finbar went to a door at the back, knocked three times and spoke the passphrase: none shall hide from the Dragon's Eye. The door opened onto a world entirely unlike the storefront that concealed it. As he crossed the threshold Finbar let his disguise fall, and Jasper saw the kender as he was. Inside, low light and faint music with no player in sight created an atmosphere designed for private conversations. Tables were spaced wide enough that neighbors could not overhear. A few nodded at Finbar as he passed. A handful did not acknowledge him at all.

At a table in the back sat a man dressed in luxurious clothing, gold earrings catching the dim light. Jasper recognized him as the figure he had spotted in the council chambers, the one who had shimmered faintly with illusion. He wore no disguise now. The man looked Jasper over with the measured calm of someone accustomed to commanding every room he entered. He said he had not been told of any other Dragon's Eye coming through Kalaman, and he asked what brought Jasper into his city and who had sent him. Lucky, Jasper answered. He tried to pass off his interest in the Tower of Wayreth as curiosity, and the man saw through it without effort. Deception was a useful tool in the guild, he said, for those who could wield it well, and he would ask only once more. Jasper told him the truth. Lucky had sent him after the Orb of Dragonkind.

The man found that interesting. Lucky had sent Jasper to infiltrate the tower and had not run it by him. Jasper admitted that he had no plan yet and could not say why Lucky wanted the Orb. Then Lucky must be truly angry with him, the man judged, to set him a task so reckless, and he suggested that Jasper not fail. He asked how long Jasper meant to stay in his city. A couple of days, Jasper said. Good, came the answer. Keep it to that, and cause no trouble.

There was a slight twitch in the man's eye when Lucky's name was spoken. Jasper noticed it but could not read what it meant. He asked how the man knew Lucky. Lucky was their guild master, he was told, and rarely recruited anyone as freely as he had recruited Jasper, which meant Jasper had badly crossed him. The man led the guild's branch in Kalaman, one of several, and reported to Lucky alone. While Jasper was in the city, he could report here. Jasper asked for any advice about the task. The man did not even know where the tower was. Jasper's best lead was Wyhan, the resident mage of High Sorcery, the same apothecary who had taken his scroll. Getting into the tower might prove easier than getting out. It was guarded by wizards, and wizards were more secretive than the guild itself. He suggested that Jasper plan his way out, if he had a way in.

He told Finbar to escort Jasper out, and Finbar rolled his eyes a little. Only then did Jasper ask the man's name. Seredyn, he said, head of the Kalaman branch. Whatever Jasper did, it was not to come back to him. Finbar stood him a drink before he left. The patrons were careful what they said around a new face, and Jasper marked the bar as a place he could return to for information.

Kakashi had gone into the city not long after Jasper, hooded, to see whether Wyhan's Apothecary was still open. The windows were dark. He knocked until Wyhan came to the door. She was closed, she told him, and he could come back in the morning with gold, and lots of it. Afterward he put on the Armor of the Fallen and gave it his full attention. It hung loose on him at first. Then it drew in and fitted itself to him, and he felt it bind to his soul. He understood that if he died, the armor would be lost with him.

At the camp, Mayor Raven was still awake and keeping an eye on things. She told Aratos that no one was scouting through the night, because most of Vogler's able bodies had been lost. Kakashi's raven had been destroyed, and he lacked what he needed to call it back. So Aratos coaxed a bird to his hand with a little food and asked it to warn him and his friends of danger. It flew off calling to the others, and he could not tell whether it had understood.

The night passed in watches. Roshi and Jasper took the first, and Mayor Raven thanked them and went to her rest. Jasper had said nothing to the others about where he had gone. Roshi mentioned that she had noticed him step away from the fire, and he answered only that he had visited an old friend, one he had not known was in Kalaman until they arrived. She listened, and sensed that he was not telling her everything. She had thought the group was finally building toward trust, and there still seemed to be a distance among them. She kept that thought to herself.

Aratos and Mayriel took the second watch, and the conversation moved from visions to legacies to the question of whether either of them would survive what was coming. Aratos asked whether she had had any more visions. She had had none since the river, where the Dragon Queen had told her the sword would find her, and the silence felt suspicious to her. He suggested that a sword could mean someone who wields one, and wondered whether the party itself might be her sword. Then he told her about his own inheritance. His ancestors had been given visions of warning and had done nothing, and he was trying to correct that wrong. He felt himself being pushed to choose between two paths from his ancestry, one of fire and one of ice. Both were good paths, he said. He did not feel worthy of either. His ancestors had been great people, and he thought it might be better to choose a path of his own. He was not very good at talking, he admitted. Mayriel did not argue with him.

Kakashi took the last watch alone, in the dark hours before morning. Early in it he noticed a figure moving through the camp toward the gates. It was Darrett, on his way to the castle, where he had been told to report for training under Marshal Vendri. He could have slept at the castle and had decided to stay with his family in the camp. The young squire wore Becklin's armor, polished and unscathed. Kakashi asked him to keep a watchful eye and to listen for anything unusual. Darrett agreed and walked on into the city.

Kakashi went on through the camp to see that all was well, and caught his reflection in a pail of water. In the water he saw the moons behind him, full and bright, all three of them, though the sky above did not show them so. The water rippled, and the face looking back at him was no longer his own. An elven woman had taken its place. Her eyes were frightened, and she glanced back and forth as if watching for something. She stood at a window that looked out on those same full moons, inside a round structure. I have to keep them safe, she said. I have to make sure they're okay. Then she turned and descended a circular staircase into a room lined with altars. Each altar held an object, some set with gemstones, some pulsing faintly with magic. She began casting a spell of protection over them. As she moved back she seemed to look straight at Kakashi, and she said: you have to fight. Keep them safe. Fire erupted, and the vision ended.

The pail held only his own reflection. Kakashi looked up, and the moons overhead were not the three full ones from the water. He did not know the woman or the place. He had learned enough of High Sorcery to know that its towers were built for that purpose: they were places of study where powerful magical items were kept safe. He kept the vision in mind and said nothing.

Morning brought the camp back to life. Marshal Vendri wanted to see the party at some point that day to make their arrangement official, and until then the day was their own. Aratos went up to the castle to train. The other four went into Kalaman as the city was waking, and Kakashi led them straight to Wyhan's Apothecary. Wyhan sat inside in a black gown with raven feathers for sleeves, and said she had known he would come. She received him with the dry tolerance she extended to all wizards who came to her with more questions than gold. He bought components for the ritual that would restore his familiar. He asked whether she had any spell scrolls worth acquiring. She named a price well beyond what he carried. When he asked for advice for a wizard in training, she told him to find a job. Altruism would only get him so far.

He had one more question. What would a wizard protect with a numerical code? A wizard would encrypt his spells, Wyhan answered, so that not just anyone could understand them. Kakashi kept the parchment out of sight, since Fizban's name was on it, and recited the numbers from memory. Wyhan judged that the numbers were there to decipher a portion of the recipe itself, and that the result would likely be the incantation that cast the spell. Roshi, listening, took the paper back and began counting out words.

The others browsed what the apothecary offered. The party's shared gold was thin, and the prices for potions were steep enough to make the choice clear. Mayriel bought two healer's kits, practical tools for stabilizing the fallen without expending divine power. Kakashi asked whether Wyhan might have work for him in the future, since he plainly needed gold. She allowed that she could perhaps use a hand or two. Then she asked them to leave her establishment, and a gust of wind rose inside the shop and ushered them gently toward the door.

Aratos's morning had not been peaceful.

He had gone to the training yard beside Kalaman's castle, where Marshal Vendri was overseeing drills and Darrett was among the trainees. The one left without a partner was Roberto, a squire Aratos had spoken with the day before. Aratos took up a practice sword and asked him to spar. They agreed on three hits. Roberto was readable, and Aratos turned his strikes aside and landed two of his own. Then Aratos grew careless and missed badly, and Roberto landed a hit.

It was the first time Aratos had been hit in a long while. At the impact a heat rose in him. It started at his center and spread outward through his skin. He dropped the practice sword and went for the Emberwake axe, which he had left to one side, with a need he could not control. The flames curling up from the blade were real, and everyone around him had become a threat in his mind. He swung.

The axe came down on Roberto. There was a cry of pain, and the squire fell and did not move. Marshal Vendri ran at Aratos and tried to seize him, and Aratos threw him off. Darrett came next, calling to Aratos and asking what he was doing, and caught the arm that held the axe. The rage turned on Darrett. Aratos tried to wrench his arm free to strike, and Darrett held on and begged him to stop. It took a moment before the flames subsided and the heat drew back into his chest. Aratos was left standing in the training yard as if he had watched himself do a thing he did not understand. He dropped the axe and ran to Roberto. Other soldiers moved to block him, and parted only enough for him to see. Roberto was alive but unconscious.

Vendri, hand on his blade, asked what that had been. Aratos answered honestly and without excuse. When Roberto hit him, a heat had risen in him, and before he knew it the axe was in his hand and he had struck. He could not control it and remembered it only as a haze. He felt that it had come from the axe, a weapon taken from an enemy he had felled and had not understood. He said he understood that the party and the Voglerans had been through a difficult time, that Roberto seemed to be all right, and that he would overlook this for the moment. Aratos thanked him, took responsibility for what he had done, and said he would reflect on the weapon.

He apologized to Darrett and to the others in the yard, and asked Darrett to carry the axe so that he would not have to hold it again. Darrett bent to lift it. Before Aratos knew he had moved, he had taken it back. He walked a little way off and thought about throwing the axe away, and found that he could not part with it. The presence in it felt foreign and familiar at once, and part of him wanted to keep it. Before he left, Vendri told him to cool off, and said he wished to see Aratos and his party when they were available.

Aratos found the others at the apothecary just as the wind put them out the door. Roshi had worked out the incantation and offered it to Kakashi, as the wizard. He left the choice to her. She spoke the words: blade made bright, let emerald fire arise, strike one foe, then leap before their eyes. The parchment stirred. Green flames rose from the page and leapt outward, touching each of the five companions in turn before the paper burned away entirely. It left each of them a new cantrip, Green Flame Blade, Aratos included: a gift from Fizban, hidden in a recipe for stew.

Aratos looked distraught. He thanked his friends for the new weapon and said they had a problem with the axe. He needed to trust them with this, he said, because they should not trust him with it. He told them everything: the sparring match, the hit, the haze, the fallen squire, and how hard Darrett and the marshal had worked to hold him. He sensed a presence in the axe that wanted no one else to hold it, wanted him unhurt, and wanted him to go on fighting and raging. It felt like his own rage, only uncontrolled, as it had been when he was first learning. He could not part with the axe, and he did not know what to do.

Kakashi, drawing on what he understood of magical theory, recognized the signs. Some items should not be used, and some bind themselves to the people who carry them. Such items were generally cursed. There were likely spells that could remove a curse, though none that he knew, and they were most often worked by clerics and paladins. He shared that assessment plainly with Roshi and Mayriel. There might be a spell they could use in the future to help Aratos.

Roshi asked to try holding the axe, to see whether it would allow a friend. Aratos reached out to give it to her and pulled it back. He wanted to, he told her, and he could not. So the party settled what they could. There would be no more sparring for Aratos for the time being, and he would go nowhere alone. One of them had a spell that could leave a man helpless with laughter, and offered to use it if the rage came again.

Aratos turned inward to see whether he could fight the presence himself. Among the five of them he saw a sixth figure standing, hooded in green: the king. Don't let it overtake you, the king said.

Then he remembered that Marshal Vendri had asked for all of them, and the party went up to the castle together. Vendri received them in his office, with Darrett present. He gave Aratos a nod that asked whether he was all right, and Aratos answered that they were working on it. Roberto, Vendri said, was stabilized and a little shaken, and would be fine.

Vendri thanked them for coming and moved on to the business at hand. The party had offered their service to Kalaman, and Kalaman had a mission for them. He needed help tracking down an inventor, a tinkerer named Tatina Rookledust, who was a few days' travel from the city. Darrett had told him that the dragon the party fought at Vogler was a machine, and Vendri believed she might be able to give them some insight into it. Most of his own men were already dispatched on other assignments. If the party was willing and able, he would appreciate their help. They accepted.

One of them asked what the mission would pay. Vendri reminded them that he had offered a stipend and they had declined it, and said he was open to revisiting that. Aratos explained the difficulty. A Knight Radiant could hold no allegiance to a single order, though he could take pay for services. Vendri answered that he needed to put something down in the record. Darrett spoke up and offered to stand as the party's proxy, so that any obligation would be his to bear. Vendri accepted the arrangement, and the party agreed to the stipend: five gold a week for each of them.

Vendri asked what the party called itself, and they gave him the name they had chosen: the Embers. A little on the nose, he said, and wrote it down. He asked them to leave as soon as they were able. Aratos asked whether he could do anything about a cursed axe. The arcane was not his strength, Vendri said, though some in Kalaman would understand more than he did. He asked whether the axe had been the cause of that morning's outburst, and Aratos said that it was. Vendri promised to see what he could find, and warned that Kalaman was not a city much inclined to magic. He sent them off with two healing potions, a map and the direction they needed.

Before they left, Aratos asked to go back to the reliquary. It was where he had felt closest to his lineage, and he wanted to learn whether the axe would feel any different there. He asked his companions to come with him, and they told him he would not be going alone. The party packed for the road and made the cave their first stop.

The cave was unchanged. The oath-tested door, marked with the symbol of the Knights Radiant, opened at the remembered sequence, and the party went down past the murals into the tomb of Sir Cthondor. Aratos moved to the center of the chamber, before the statue, and closed his eyes. He took out the Emberwake axe and felt the presence well up again. He asked for help in overcoming the curse, so that he could keep his oaths as a Knight Radiant and protect those who could not protect themselves. The king appeared once more, standing before him in the tomb, and laid a hand on his shoulder. You must test your resolve.

The fire came immediately. The others saw flames rise along the axe and felt the heat coming off him. It rose from the axe into his arm and moved toward his chest, and the rage began to build again until he saw red. Then he remembered the tree, the fire and the ice, and he chose resolve. It snuffed out the flame. A chill ran from his chest down through his arm and into the weapon itself, pushing the rage down. The rough handle and unadorned blade shifted into the weapon he had taken up in his vision. For a moment he held the Leviathan Axe. Then it faded back to the Emberwake.

The king was gone. Aratos looked at the statue and felt a calm come over him. When he turned around, his companions stood ready with weapons drawn, and Jasper was watching from the shadows. They had seen a brief mist rise from him as the axe changed shape and changed back. He told them that he knew now. He thought he could control this, and he would have to be ready. He would do it.

From the cave the Embers took the road. Tatina Rookledust was a few days away, and they had the map and the direction in hand.
