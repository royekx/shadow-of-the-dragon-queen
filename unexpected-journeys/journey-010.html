Unexpected Journey 010

The Weight of the Axe

A recipe hides a spell. A trophy hides a curse.
UJ 010 Party Level 4 Kalaman

Aratos's trophy axe takes him in a sparring match and nearly kills a squire; at the reliquary he masters it once through restraint. Marshal Vendri sends the party, now named the Embers, to find the tinkerer Tatina Rookledust.

The evening after the reliquary settled gently over the refugee camp, and for a little while the world felt almost quiet. Fizban's stew had done its work. The people of Vogler moved through the firelit dark with something lighter in their steps, and the sound of voices carried warmth rather than grief. Fizban himself sat at the fire with his pipe, nodding in and out of sleep with the contented air of a man who had done exactly what he intended. Mayor Raven made her rounds and offered her goodnights. Darrett Highwater arrived late, covered in the sweat of a long day's training, and took his rest among the Voglerans rather than behind Kalaman's walls. The choice was a small one, but it meant something.

As Fizban finally roused himself to shuffle into the city, a scrap of parchment fell from where he had been sitting and drifted to the ground unnoticed. Roshi retrieved it. The paper turned out to be a recipe — Ispin Greenshield's campfire stew, recorded in Fizban's hand — and at the bottom, a note to himself that the last time he had attempted the dish, he had forgotten certain key ingredients, with results he had found educational. The recipe listed its components in absurd quantities, each line bearing a numerical value that seemed excessive for any ordinary stew. When Roshi shared it with the others, Kakashi studied the numbers and recognized something beneath the surface. Wizards were known to hide their spells in plain sight, encoding incantations within innocuous texts so that only those who knew how to read them could draw out the power within. The numbers, he suspected, were not measurements at all.

The recipe was set aside for the moment. There was still an evening to get through.

Aratos sat apart from the others, sharpening his weapons by firelight in the methodical way of a man who needed his hands busy while his mind worked. As the stone moved against steel, the campfire shifted. Flames bent and gathered into the shape of a great tree, its branches burning on one side and glazed with frost and ice on the other. At the base of the tree, a small black rose bloomed for just a moment before vanishing. Beyond the vision of the tree, a figure stood in the distance, wearing a dark green hood. His bearing was that of a man carrying authority he did not advertise. Gruff-bearded, and carrying himself as a man hiding his own regality. Aratos rose and walked toward him.

The king — for Aratos recognized him as one of the ancestral spirits he had glimpsed before — spoke only a few words. The same blood flows through my veins. The same weakness. Don't let it take you. Then he faded. Aratos stood in the grass where the figure had been and found no impression, no mark, no sign that anything had stood there. He returned to his weapons and his contemplation, the Emberwake axe warming faintly in his hand.

Jasper, meanwhile, had noticed Finbar watching from the edge of camp. The older man stood just outside the firelight, invisible to everyone else, but the shimmer around him was familiar enough that Jasper caught it. He slipped away from the group without ceremony and made his way toward the figure. Finbar dropped his invisibility as Jasper approached, and his face was not the weathered face of Old Man Jenkins any longer. He was simply himself: a man of slightly tanned skin and quiet authority, who moved through Kalaman's gates as someone the guards had seen before. He led Jasper into the city.

The streets were calm in the late evening, lit by moonlight and the occasional lamp. Finbar brought Jasper to an establishment whose sign read Peller's Provisions, a general goods store that showed every sign of being closed for the night. But when Jasper looked closely at the sign, a shimmer resolved the name into something else: Finn's Bar. Finbar knocked three times and spoke the passphrase, and the door opened onto a world entirely unlike the storefront that concealed it. Inside, low light and quiet music created an atmosphere designed for private conversations. Tables were spaced wide enough that neighbors could not overhear, and the people seated at them had the careful, watchful quality of those who made a profession of discretion. A few nodded at Finbar as he passed.

At a table in the back sat a man dressed in luxurious clothing, gold earrings catching the dim light. Jasper recognized him as the figure he had spotted in the council chambers, the one who had shimmered faintly with illusion. The man looked Jasper over with the measured calm of someone accustomed to commanding every room he entered. He introduced himself as Seredyn, head of the Dragon's Eye branch in Kalaman, and made clear that Lucky had not informed him of Jasper's presence or mission in the city. The conversation that followed was blunt. Jasper's attempt at misdirection was thin, and Seredyn saw through it without effort, though he received the truth about the task — stealing the Orb of Dragonkind from the Tower of Wayreth — with something between surprise and resignation. He confirmed that the local apothecary, Wyhan, was Jasper's best lead to the tower, and offered one piece of counsel that carried real weight: getting into the tower might prove easier than getting out. It was guarded by wizards, and wizards were more secretive than the guild itself.

There was a slight twitch at the corner of Seredyn's eye when Lucky's name was spoken, brief and quickly controlled. Jasper noticed it but could not read what it meant. Seredyn closed the meeting with a simple instruction: keep the visit short, cause no trouble, and ensure that nothing came back to him. Finbar escorted Jasper out, and Jasper stayed long enough to take the drink offered at the bar before returning to camp. He said nothing to the others about where he had gone. When Roshi asked during the night watch, he answered only that he had visited an old friend. She listened, and sensed that he was holding something back, and sat with that feeling in silence.

The night passed in watches. Aratos spoke quietly with Mayriel during their shared turn, and the conversation moved from visions to legacies to the question of whether either of them would survive what was coming. Mayriel mentioned that she had not received a vision in some time, and that the silence felt suspicious to her. The Dragon Queen had told her the sword would find her, and she did not know whether that meant a weapon or the person who wielded one. Aratos told her something of his own inheritance: the history of his family, the ancestor who had failed to heed a warning and brought ruin, the two paths he felt pulling at him. He said he did not feel worthy of either legacy. He was not very good at talking, he admitted. Mayriel did not argue with him.

Kakashi's watch came later, in the deepest part of the night. He was making a quiet circuit of the camp when he caught his reflection in a pail of water left near one of the tents. The moons were not visible in the sky at that hour, but in the water's surface they appeared full and bright, all three of them. The face looking back at him was not entirely his own. A woman's face replaced his in the reflection — an elven woman with frightened eyes, glancing back and forth as if watching for something. She stood before a window that looked out on those same full moons. Then she turned and descended a circular staircase into a room lined with altars. Each altar held an object that pulsed faintly with magical energy. She began casting a spell of protection over them, her movements urgent and deliberate. Then she looked up, and her eyes found Kakashi's through the water, and she said: you have to fight. Keep them safe. Fire erupted, and the vision ended.

Kakashi stood over the pail for a moment, then looked up at the dark sky. The moons were not there. He continued his watch and said nothing, but the image of that circular room and its carefully guarded relics stayed with him. He had learned enough of High Sorcery to know that its towers were built precisely for that purpose — to keep powerful magical items safe from those who would misuse them. The vision seemed to be telling him something about that. He filed it away and kept walking.

Not long before dawn, Kakashi noticed a figure moving quietly through camp. It was Darrett, heading for the castle training grounds before the sun had properly risen. The young squire wore Becklin's armor, polished and unscathed, and carried himself with the careful posture of someone still growing into a responsibility he had not chosen but would not refuse. They exchanged a few words, and then Darrett walked on into the city.

Morning brought the camp back to life and the party back to Kalaman. Kakashi made his way to Wyhan's Apothecary as soon as it opened, and was received with the dry tolerance the apothecary extended to all wizards who came to her with more questions than gold. He purchased components for the ritual that would restore his familiar, and asked whether she had any spell scrolls worth acquiring. She named a price well beyond what he carried. He asked instead about the recipe — reciting the numerical values from Fizban's parchment without producing the paper itself — and Wyhan confirmed what he had suspected. The numbers were a cipher. They pointed to specific words within the recipe itself, and those words, taken in order, formed an incantation. The spell hidden within the stew recipe was called Green Flame Blade.

The others browsed what the apothecary offered. Roshi and Mayriel purchased healer's kits, practical tools for stabilizing the fallen without expending divine power. The party's shared gold was thin, and the prices for potions were steep enough to make the choice clear.

Outside the apothecary, with Aratos having rejoined the group, Roshi studied the recipe once more and spoke the incantation she had worked out from its hidden structure. The words came out as something between an invocation and a riddle: blade made bright, let emerald fire arise, strike one foe, then leap before their eyes. The parchment stirred. Green flames rose from the page and leapt outward, touching each of the five companions in turn before the paper burned away entirely. The fire left no mark and caused no pain. What it left instead was understanding — a new cantrip, shared among all of them, a parting gift from Fizban encoded in something as ordinary as a recipe for stew.

The morning might have continued peacefully from there. It did not.

Aratos had gone to the training yard at Kalaman's castle to spar with the other soldiers, hoping to keep his edge sharp while the party waited for their next assignment. Roberto, a squire he had met briefly the day before, was among the trainees, and the two squared off with practice blades in a straightforward bout. The sparring went well enough at first. Roberto was readable but capable, and Aratos parried his strikes with ease. Then Roberto landed a hit.

It was a single blow, nothing that should have mattered. But something rose in Aratos at the impact — a heat that started deep and spread quickly, moving from his center outward through his limbs. Before he fully understood what was happening, the practice sword was on the ground and the Emberwake axe was in his hand. He did not remember choosing to reach for it. The flames curling up from the blade were real, and the figures around him had become threats in his mind, every one of them. He swung.

The axe came down on Roberto. The squire fell and did not rise. Marshal Vendri ran toward Aratos, and Darrett moved to hold back his arm, both of them calling out as the rage burned through him. It took time and effort before the heat finally receded, leaving Aratos standing in the middle of the training yard with a fallen man at his feet and no clean memory of the moments between the first blow and the last. He dropped the axe and went to Roberto. The other soldiers moved to block him. Roberto was alive, unconscious and badly hurt, but alive.

Vendri heard Aratos out. The explanation was honest and offered without excuse: the axe had triggered something he could not control, something that felt like his barbarian rage but wilder and more foreign, as if it came from outside him rather than within. Vendri accepted the account with the measured patience of a man who had seen strange things before. Roberto would recover. The incident would be overlooked, for now. But Vendri's eyes stayed on the Emberwake axe as he said it.

When Aratos rejoined the party, the axe went with him. He tried to hand it to Darrett and found that his arm would not complete the motion. He tried to set it down deliberately and walked back to it without deciding to. The curse was not subtle about what it wanted. He told his companions everything — the sparring match, the rage, the fallen squire, the inability to part with the weapon. Kakashi, drawing on what he understood of magical theory, recognized the signs. Cursed items of this nature were not easily broken by ordinary means. The removal of such a binding generally required divine magic, the kind wielded by clerics or paladins. He shared that assessment plainly, and Roshi and Mayriel understood what it meant. The cure might lie within their own power, in time.

For the moment, the party focused on what they could do. Aratos asked to visit the reliquary before they left the city, and the others agreed to accompany him. He would not go alone, and they would not let him.

The cave was unchanged. The oath-tested door opened at the remembered sequence, and the party descended into the tomb of Sir Cthondor. Aratos moved to the center of the chamber and knelt before the statue. He held the Emberwake axe and asked the honored dead for help — not to be freed of the weapon, but to be shown how to carry it without being consumed by it. The ancestral king appeared once more, standing before him in the quiet of the tomb, and laid a hand on his shoulder. You must test your resolve.

The fire came immediately. It rose from the axe into his arm and moved toward his chest, and the rage began to build again. Aratos felt it gathering and did not fight it with strength. He chose the other path. He thought of the tree — fire on one side, ice on the other — and reached for the cold side. He chose stillness over fury. The heat met something immovable inside him and could not pass it. The flames along the axe died down. A chill ran from his chest down through his arm and into the weapon itself, and for a moment the Emberwake axe changed. The rough handle and unadorned blade shifted into something else entirely — a weapon from the vision he had carried since the beginning of this road, transformed by the choice he had just made. It held that shape for only a moment before returning to what it had been.

But the moment had been real. The party had seen the mist rise and the axe change and revert. They had seen Aratos come back from the edge on his own terms.

He rose from the floor of the tomb and looked at his companions. He said he thought he could do it. That he would need to be ready, and that he would need their help when he was not. The party did not argue. They simply stood with him in the quiet of the reliquary and let the moment be what it was.

When they emerged from the cave and made their way back to Kalaman, Marshal Vendri was waiting for them. He acknowledged Aratos's state with a brief look and moved on to the business at hand. The party had offered their service to Kalaman, and Kalaman had a mission for them. A tinkerer named Tatina Rookledust lived several days' travel from the city. The mechanical dragon that had burned Vogler was a remarkable and dangerous piece of engineering, and Vendri believed she might be able to shed light on its construction and the forces behind it. Most of his available soldiers were already committed elsewhere. The Embers were the right people for the task.

Darrett spoke up before the conversation could turn to the question of formal allegiance. He offered again to serve as the party's proxy, taking any official obligations onto himself so that the Embers could operate without being formally conscripted. Vendri accepted the arrangement. He asked for a name to put in the record, and the party gave him the one they had chosen for themselves: the Embers. Vendri wrote it down without comment. He also promised to look into what resources Kalaman might have for addressing a cursed weapon, though he acknowledged that the city was not particularly well-stocked with arcane remedies. He sent them off with two healing potions and the direction they needed.

The party gathered their things, settled their accounts with the city, and walked out through Kalaman's gates as the morning stretched toward midday. The road ahead was several days long, and the dangers along it were not yet known. Behind them, the refugee camp still stood along the river, the people of Vogler making the best of what they had been given. Darrett would look after them. Fizban had said he would be in the city a little while longer before moving on to whatever came next for him.

The Embers had a name now, and a mission, and a road. Aratos carried the Emberwake axe at his side, its fire banked but not gone. Somewhere ahead of them, a tinkerer waited.
