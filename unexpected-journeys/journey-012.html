Unexpected Journey 012

The Three Gifts

Three gifts taken back from the village, and a healer who had been feeding on it.
UJ 012 Party Level 4 Bracken Hollow
Previous JourneyAll Journeys
With two travelers from Barrowmere, the bard Jumanji and the tinkerer Tien, the Embers find the three things that bind Bracken Hollow: a black seed under Gribb Brack's field, a white tooth that kept Sela alive after the Pale Fever, and the red banner Becklin gave Boyd in the Blood Summer. Sela dies when the tooth is taken. On the hill the monument rises again and takes Jasper to the cottage of Marigold, the village healer, where he breaks her charms. Roshi's shield wakes, Kakashi sends the monument away, and Aratos lets go of the axe's fury. The gifts are seated beneath the hill and the spell breaks. Marigold vanishes, and the party splits: Roshi and Kakashi to Kalaman with the guests, Aratos, Mayariel and Jasper on to Tatina Rookledust.

From the window of a crowded house in Bracken Hollow, where the villagers huddled together for warmth and safety through the dangerous hours, three people watched the monument fall. The ground shook with it. Sergeant Boyd, an older man with gray hair and dark skin, stared at the hill for a long moment. He could hardly believe that anyone had killed the thing. Then he took up his sword and told the two travelers beside him to come along. He meant to see it for himself.

The two had been in the village about a week. Jumanji was a bard, very tall and broad, with a flute at his hip and an easy way with a tune. Tien was small and quick, a tinkerer hung all over with gadgets, who worked her magic through a set of thimbles. They had come from their own village of Barrowmere carrying a letter to Kalaman, and like everyone else on the road they had walked into Bracken Hollow and found they could not walk out again. In that week they had become the sort of people Boyd turned to when something needed doing. They searched for frostlace as they climbed, because word had already gone round that the newcomers were looking for it, and found none. It would grow on lower ground, if it grew anywhere.

At the top of the hill the strangers were picking themselves up among the broken stone. The introductions were made there in the strange light. Aratos Stormblessed, a half-elf of Solamnia, had only just come back to his senses, and he was working his jaw where Roshi had struck him to bring him down. Jasper Quickwit, hooded and sleepy-eyed, gave his name as though it were a joke he was in on. Kakashi said he looked old but was not so very old, and as the newcomers looked at him, his eyes changed for a moment to a red iris split by a black slit, and then were ordinary again. Roshi, tall and ageless, admitted to being a good deal older than she looked. Boyd nodded at all of it. These were the ones who had come in that night and gone straight up the hill.

Aratos told them what the party knew. The monument was dead, but no one could leave yet. The curse still held, and three things had to be found to break it, though nobody knew what they looked like or where they were. Roshi went through the dangers of each moon for the newcomers, and pointed out that Kakashi could see the magic on things that mattered. Kakashi asked them all to keep an eye out for frostlace, which he needed for an errand of his own. When the guests asked why, the party was careful in its answer. It was Kakashi's business. Roshi was frank with them about one more thing: the party knew that Boyd held the village together, but they did not yet know what sort of leader he was.

Boyd gave them two names. Old Gribb Brack had lived in the village nearly since its founding, and his farm was what kept it fed. If anyone knew where frostlace grew, it was Gribb. The other was Marigold, the village grandmother, a healer who knew about curiosities. Her cottage stood just past the edge of the village, but nobody had reached it since the curse closed. A person could see it plainly and walk toward it all day, and the path would only bring them round again. They decided to split up. Roshi, Kakashi and Jasper would go to the farm. Aratos and Mayariel would go into the village with Boyd, and Jumanji and Tien, who trusted Boyd above anyone, went with them.

First they slept, for a few hours, as well as anyone could sleep in that place. When they woke the white moon had risen. A light sickness lay over everything, and the echoes were out again: pale figures of villagers living and gone, carrying baskets and hanging out linens, going about some ordinary day that had ended long ago.

At the Brack farm, Roshi's sharp eyes picked out the one living soul among the echoes: an old man in a rocking chair on the porch. Out in the fields, ghostly hands were tilling the soil, and one echo was digging a hole. Roshi went up to the porch and told the old man plainly what they wanted, which was to break the cycle and let the village go free, and to find frostlace along the way. Gribb Brack had lived there for eighty-four years. He told them that nothing grew under the white moon or the red. The crops came up only under the black, and everything died again as the moon passed. The village had perhaps four hours to harvest, cook and eat before it all withered. If they wanted frostlace, they would have to wait for the black moon, fight off the wolves, and hope they did not die. He had none put by.

Jasper, as the others were used to, had already slipped away. He watched one echo in particular, a younger Gribb by the look of him, walking quickly in from the edge of town with something held close against his chest. The echo kept away from the others laughing over their work, chose a corner of the field that nobody was tending, dug a small hole, buried what he carried, patted the earth down, and walked off. Jasper went to the spot and dug with his dagger. Under a thin layer of soil were roots that pulsed with light, and he called the other two over.

Kakashi knelt over the roots and read them. They ran down to something deeper, and the magic in them drew on life rather than giving it. The three dug carefully until they uncovered the source of the pulse: a knot as black as jet, the size of a stone, shaped like a seed, that would have looked rotten anywhere else. Roshi was unwilling to harm the plant. They settled on cutting the roots and keeping the seed whole. When they lifted it free, a wave of cold washed over them.

Two wolves rose out of the ground like black water. Roshi met the first with her moon-touched sword, glowing bright under the white moon, and the wound she gave it would have dropped one of the wolves they had fought the night before. This one stayed on its feet. She understood that the seed was feeding them and told Jasper to run with it, and he slipped away behind a shed. Kakashi circled the wounded wolf through the mud, nimble for an old man, and drove an arc of lightning into it with a shout. The lightning burst out through its mouth and eyes, and it fell to ash. From behind the shed Jasper looked up and saw the black moon sliding across the white. He steadied his bow, trusted Roshi to hold her ground, and shot the second wolf past her. As it died, the black moon locked over the white, and a chorus of howling rose across the village. It came from the hill. The seed sat strangely in Jasper's hand, but it did him no harm, and it was about the size of a recess.

In the village, the others found Ked sitting bandaged on the porch of the house where the sick were tended. A faint tint of red and black still clung to him, the mark of someone to be taken at the next eclipse. The village around him was divided between hope and the weariness of people who had hoped before. When the party tried to speak to an echo laying out linens, it paid them no attention, and a hand passed through it like mist. A villager told them they had all tried that. The echoes never answered.

The woman who kept the sickhouse came out to meet them. Her name was Sela. She was young, perhaps thirty, and she had heard they killed the giant, and wanted to know whether that meant everyone could leave. She offered to patch them up. A man's voice from inside called her back to rest. Mayariel noticed something then that no one else had: everyone in the village moved a little dully under the white moon, but Sela did not. She looked perfectly well.

Inside among the cots, Aratos let her see to his jaw. She had never set one before, but she thought she had watched Marigold do it, and she put it back with a sharp twist while he held still. She spoke warmly of Marigold. The old healer used to come by to look in on people, and during the fever last winter she had helped everyone. No one had seen her since the village closed. Sela could think of no one who had wronged her. She had kept to herself, like a grandmother to the whole village. When Mayariel remarked that Sela looked very well for someone in this place, Sela's answer came sharply: she had not made this, if that was what Mayariel meant.

The man who had called her was her husband, Miren, a carpenter with brown hair and a long beard, who had built the house and kept it in order. He came out to ask whether these people were troubling his wife. Jumanji lifted his flute and played a few easy notes, and Miren's shoulders loosened. He was no man for the arcane, he said. Only Marigold had been. People went to her with things that were beyond the rest of them, and some said she granted them things. When Jumanji pressed him further, Miren flinched and turned the talk aside. Their friend looked better now. Perhaps it was time they were going.

Tien tried a different door. She flattered him as one builder to another, a little new to the trade herself, and asked whether he would look at Aratos's axe, whose head she claimed was loose. Miren took the two of them into his workshop at the back and bent over the Emberwake, which was not loose at all. While his back was turned, Tien tipped one of her thimbles and felt for magic. Something was pulsing in the room they had just left. Aratos gave her a nod, and she slipped back out. Aratos stayed, and asked Miren the question that had been sitting on him. When he used the axe he felt a pull, a strange presence in it. Miren could feel nothing. He told Aratos to find Marigold, or a wizard or a cleric, and then, kindly enough, told him that if he had something to get off his chest he should say it, and not bottle it up.

In the main room Jumanji turned his tune on Sela, and she grew easier with him. Miren was very protective, she said, and with reason. When the Pale Fever came last winter, most people recovered. She had not. She had grown very sick, and after she got better he had become more careful with her. Tien found what she was looking for at Sela's throat: a small crescent of ivory on a cord, glowing faintly. Miren had given it to her during the winter. Tien studied it closely and felt healing in it, holy in character. It was bone, a gnarled and curving tooth, most likely a hobgoblin's tusk. She asked if she might try it on, and said she would like to buy one like it. Sela refused gently. Her husband had given it to her, and she had known these two only a week.

For a moment the guests talked of simply taking it. Sela was an ordinary young woman who had done them no wrong, and they chose the truth instead, or most of it. They told her that the pendant might save the village. Tien had felt it pulsing, and Sela alone stood untouched by the moon's sickness, and that was too much to be chance. Sela wanted the village saved as much as anyone. She only could not see how a gift from her husband could do it. Miren came back and told them flatly that it was time to go, and Boyd, hearing the raised voices, stepped in and looked to the two of them for leave. Tien tapped the pendant and made it flash white, then red, then black, to show Sela that it belonged to the moons. Aratos and Jumanji each laid a hand on Miren's shoulders, and he went still.

If it would help the village, Sela said, then of course. They promised to bring it back if it did not. Miren cried out to her not to do it and fought against the hands that held him, but they held. Sela lifted the cord over her head and gave the pendant to Tien.

Her body convulsed at once. Her neck twisted and she screamed as the color ran out of her skin. Her hair went gray, her eyes sank and burned, and her skin drew tight over her bones. What had been Sela lunged at Tien with long nails and missed. Jumanji tried to hold her still with a word, and she threw it off. Tien hesitated. This was the woman who had been tending the sick of the village all month. Then she raised a thimble and loosed a ray of frost, and Sela's body seized, fell to the floor, and did not move again. Oh no, Tien said. She had not wanted to kill her.

Miren tore free, struck Aratos across the face, and fell to his knees beside his wife. What had they done? They had killed her. Aratos told him that he believed she had already been dead. Miren's eyes dropped, and the truth came out of him. When the Pale Fever came, everyone else got better, and Sela did not. He had heard that Marigold could help with things like that. She gave him the pendant, and when he put it on Sela, she recovered. And then these strangers came, and now she was gone. Boyd put a hand on his shoulder, and Miren swatted it away and asked only to be left alone. The party took the pendant and went out while the villagers gathered around him. On the step, Boyd said quietly that he hoped they were right about this. If they were not, he did not know how much longer the town could hold on.

Outside the white light had grown stronger, and every echo in the village had turned and begun to walk toward the hill.

The two groups found each other in the square and compared what they carried. One had a seed and the other a necklace. Roshi put it together. The seed had brought the wolves, so it belonged to the black moon, and the pendant belonged to the white. The last thing would belong to the red, the moon of the fighting. Boyd knew what it might be. He had been in Bracken Hollow for the Blood Summer, with his wife, and his wife had not survived it. Hobgoblins had besieged the village and would not let up. In those days Bracken Hollow was the main source of food for Kalaman, because its crops were the best in the region, and the hobgoblins cut the road so that no help could come. Then Becklin came, and gave Boyd a banner, and it was flown over the gate. He did not know how they survived the fight that followed, but the hobgoblins never came back. That was twenty-seven years ago.

The banner still hung at the gate. Up close it was a grain sack with the village's mark painted on it, and Tien's magic showed it humming with power. They sent Jasper, the quietest of them, to take it down. As it came away in his hands, red wisps gathered behind him into a great hobgoblin captain with a helmet and a sword, and a tusk curved like a crescent moon. Jasper never saw it. He only felt the sword. Roshi's arrow took the captain near the heart, and it barely kept its feet. Jumanji finished it with words alone, an insult so cutting that the captain's head burst and red light seeped out of it as it came apart. Nothing was left behind, but the party had seen enough of its tusk to know the shape. It was the same as the pendant.

The moment the banner came down, the red moon swung into place, and the eclipse formed hours before its time. Everything turned red and black and white. Somewhere far off, stone ground against stone. On the hilltop the monument had made itself whole again and was waiting. Aratos reminded them what they had learned the hard way: strike the three rings on its chest, and it would come apart far faster than it had before. The plan they made was simple. Jasper would carry all three gifts and fly up unseen on his winged boots while the rest drew the monument's eye, and he would wear the pendant only if he had to.

Boyd offered to go with them or to keep order in the village, whichever they needed. The village's fighters had died in the Blood Summer, they reminded him. He was pushing sixty, he said, but he was still fit, and he had survived a month of this and the Blood Summer before it. When they asked him to come, he said that he would always lay down his life for the people he protected. On the way through the village, people stood outside their doors staring up at the moons. They all knew the timing was wrong. Some looked frightened, and some looked as if they thought the world was ending.

The hill was crawling with wolves and red hobgoblins, and with echoes drifting among them. The monument stood at the top, over the hidden cavern, and one of its rings flashed white. Kakashi raised his hands, veins crawling across them, and vines burst from the ground to wrap a hobgoblin, but it shrugged off the death in them. These creatures had been made from the same dark stuff as the seed. Aratos let the rage take him, and his ancestral spirits gathered around him as he charged uphill and cut a hobgoblin in half with his sword. Mayariel raised a sphere of gossamer silver light, and everyone inside it felt their strength bolstered. A spectral weapon flew from her hand, and Sunscale flew with her. Boyd pulled on his helmet, drew his sword, and said quietly, just like before. He ran up the slope and struck a wolf, and when it ducked his second swing, Jasper put an arrow through it. Boyd gave him an admiring glance. Roshi's sword shed its moonlight all around her, but the light of the blade and the light of the three moons fought each other, and her stroke went wide.

The monument raised its fists and slammed them into the hill. A red wave rolled down the slope, and the creatures staggered but held. It slammed again, and this time the wave was black, and Jasper felt nothing at all. Light washed over him, and he was standing in a cozy cottage with a small fire burning and an old woman in a chair. She was delighted to have guests, she said, and invited him in. Her name was Marigold. Jasper told her he had been helping his friends in a battle and had suddenly appeared there. Interesting, she said. He should make himself at home. Would he like some tea? He accepted it calmly. Only then did he notice that the gifts were no longer in his hands.

On the hill, Jasper had simply vanished, boots and all, and the seed, the pendant and the banner had fallen where he stood. Kakashi had seen the spellwork in the flash. It was a working that moved a person from one place to another, and the same weave ran through the white light the monument gave off. The black wave had taken Jasper, and they guessed it was the gifts that had marked him. Kakashi pressed up the path and threw a shard of ice at the white ring. It burst against it, and the ring went dim.

Aratos reached for another foe with his sword, and his hand would not have it. The Emberwake called, and he took it up. When he carved through the next creature, fire burst from the blade. Something had changed in him. Before, he had cut because it had to be done. Now there was pleasure in it. He knew what they had come to do and kept moving toward the monument, and the others kept their distance from him. Boyd charged a hobgoblin and cut it twice. He was a soldier, after all. Mayariel's bolt flew at the black ring and missed, but her spectral weapon struck it. Then she saw the gifts lying on the grass and picked up the pendant.

The monument slammed the ground again, and the light washed over Roshi. As it did, she remembered the story she had told at Ispin's funeral. He had insisted on hunting bears, as he always did, and got in over his head, and when the mother bear came at him, Roshi had stood in front of him and taken the blow herself. The memory surged into Ispin's green shield. As the wave tried to throw her back, vines grew out of the shield, wound down her arm and around her legs, and held her where she stood. She heard a warm chuckle that sounded very much like Ispin. The wave broke around her and did her no harm. From then on the hill could not move her.

In the cottage, Marigold poured the tea. She hoped he did not mind ginseng. When Jasper asked where he was, she said it was just her old cottage outside Bracken Hollow, where she had been for a while to take care of people. When he asked what was happening in the village, she was sure they were all going about their ordinary day, and she thought he must be tired from traveling and should rest. He told her he had friends there and had to go back. She did not know how he could. Something on the road had taught Jasper not to drink tea from strangers, and he looked around the room instead. Her earrings and her necklace bore the same three rings he had seen on the monument's chest, white and red and black, and one of them, the red, was lit. In the corner were scraps of clothing that would have fit someone much younger. When he asked about the jewelry, she said she had made it herself, as offerings to the moons. They held a great deal of power, and she used them in healing tonics.

Jasper rose and made for the door, and a powerful wish to sit back down came over him. He sat, though it felt wrong, and kept his wits about him. There was no need to be hostile, young Jasper, Marigold said. He had never told her his name. He waited for his moment, drew his dagger and went for the glowing earring. She was quick for a woman of eighty-some years, but he was quicker, and the earring shattered. She looked at him with great sadness, waved her hand, and he was back on the hill. On the hill, at the same moment, the red ring went dead, and the hobgoblins were gone.

Jumanji picked up the seed and climbed toward the top. The next black wave seized him, and he felt his body begin to twist away somewhere else. He refused. This was his body, and he stayed. The others saw the light take hold of him and then watched him grow solid again. Tien took up the banner. A wolf leapt across her aim and spoiled her shot, and she ran on past its snapping jaws.

Then came footsteps from below, and a wolf went flying, and a spear sailed over the party's heads. The villagers had heard the fighting and come up the hill. Corvin was among them, and Ked, and men and women who had no business on a battlefield, carrying rakes and shovels, terrified and coming anyway. They rallied behind Boyd as he held his sword high, and they threw themselves at the wolves and the echoes so that the party could push for the top.

The party had learned by now that the rings could be worn down in turns. A ring that went dim would light again, but the damage stayed. Aratos hurled his javelin, and lightning reared in his hand and thunder cracked across the hill, and cracks spread through the monument's chest. Right behind his blue lightning came Mayariel's bolt, silvery gold, and it took the shape of a dragon and coiled around the stone before her spectral weapon struck after it. The black ring cracked through. Boyd, caught up in it all, threw his sword at the red ring. Jasper, back on his feet and with his boots under him, flew low over the field, gathered all three gifts from the others, and hid himself in the fog near the top. Then the monument flared black, and he was gone again, and the gifts fell to the grass. He had warned them, he said later, and they had walked into it a second time.

This time Kakashi was watching closely. He saw the lattice of spellwork wrap around Jasper and carry him off, and he understood how it worked, well enough to hold on to it. Roshi's arrow dimmed the white ring again. Then flowers wound around her and closed and fell away, leaving a bed of blossoms where she had stood, and she was thirty feet further up the hill. The next pull came for her, the nearest to the fallen gifts, and the vines grew up from her feet and rooted her to the earth, and she did not move. Tien put a thimble to her lips and blew, and a glittering dust settled over the monument and grew tiny wings, so that it was speckled with points of light like fireflies, and every blow found it more easily. She loosed a ray of frost that hardened as it flew, and it went straight through the red ring and cracked it. Only the white was left.

More wolves came, and more hobgoblins, and some of the villagers fell. Others fought on with shovels, or with nothing but their shoes. Kakashi felt a second mark open in his eye, and with it came full understanding of the working he had watched. He turned it on the monument. The power of the moons erupted out of him, and the monument was gone from the hill. The cost came at once. The spell was beyond what his body could bear, and a deep, heavy weariness settled into him.

Aratos felt the fury in him straining toward everything around him, and he refused it. This was not the time. He let the rage go, and his friends were his friends again. The villagers shouldered a hobgoblin out of his way, shouting at him to go, and he scooped up all three gifts and ran for the crest. He dropped into the cavern below. The three seats in its walls glowed faintly white and red and black. Boyd stood guard at the rim above, waiting, though he did not know for what.

Jasper had been pulled away again, and the cottage was not so pleasant this time. Marigold's earring was still broken. Then a great thump shook the ground outside, and through the window Jasper saw the monument lying in the garden. Even Marigold looked confused. Why here? Her face had changed. It was longer than before, her skin was peeling, and her ears had grown long. Everything had been fine, she said, until fools appeared. But she supposed eighty-four years of feeding was good enough. Jasper struck again and broke another of her charms. She spat at him and called him a wench and flung out her hand, and he was torn back through the space between the cottage and the hill, and as he went he saw the magic unraveling around him.

Mayariel climbed down into the cavern, took the pendant from Aratos, and set it in its seat. White light poured out, blinding. Jumanji heaved himself in after her and set the banner, and the red light outside swelled. A great rumble came from above as the monument rose again, moving as though it did not know where it was. Tien ran for the opening, dodged, and tumbled in head first. For the first time, two of the three seats were filled. She held the seed for a moment, wondering whether she should. Then she set it in place.

Light filled the cavern and shot up into the sky. It struck the inside of the dome that had sealed the village and began to peel the lattice apart. For the first time in a week the party saw the true sky. It was an ordinary cloudy day, no longer dim and sad. The moons hung there a moment longer and then faded away as the illusion came off Bracken Hollow. A voice entered all their minds, and they knew it for Marigold's. Fine, she said. She had got what she needed. Perhaps they would meet again, and they would not be as successful. Then there was only the sound of the village, everyone weeping and shouting at once and holding each other, free. Somewhere in the distance a great stone fell to the ground with a low rumble. The cottage still stood far off past the edge of the village, but Marigold was gone.

Boyd helped them out of the cavern, hardly able to believe that he was free. He thanked them all. They were welcome to stay as long as they liked, and the people would want to thank them properly, but he understood that Bracken Hollow had only been a stop on their road. If they needed to go, the village would see them supplied. He pressed a paper into their hands thanking them for their service. Then the party was paraded through the village, and as they went they saw it come back to itself. The crops were growing. Animals that had been missing all month were back in the yards and fields. They went out to the farm and spoke with the people there, and Kakashi gathered what he still needed. His list was complete.

Jumanji and Tien would go on to Kalaman, as they had always meant to, and ask for help for their own village. As the party gathered, a raven circled down and landed by Kakashi and Roshi with a note. It had been a few days since anyone had heard from them, it said. The Test of High Sorcery was postponed for now, on account of the war. And Fizban had been in the apothecary for some time, belligerently demanding the wand and the shield. Would they return them to Kalaman as urgently as they could? The writer was unsure what Fizban needed, but needed him to leave the shop alone. It was signed Wyhan.

So the company divided. Roshi and Kakashi would go west to Kalaman with Jumanji and Tien. Kakashi was more stooped than usual, still drained from the working on the hill, and it would be some days before he was himself again. Aratos, Mayariel and Jasper would go on to find Tatina Rookledust, as they had been sent to do. At the main road they shook hands all round and said their goodbyes, which none of them meant to be the last. Then the four turned toward Kalaman, and the three walked on toward the tinkerer, and behind them Bracken Hollow lay open under an ordinary sky.
